# Changelog

All notable changes to KazBars will be documented in this file.

The format is based on [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/).

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- **Overhead grids — icons that float over characters' heads.** Add Grid now offers two kinds: Floating (Player, Target — a spot on screen, as before) and Overhead (Friendly, Hostile). An overhead grid draws over each character it applies to instead of at a screen position: Friendly over you (switch on **Over me**), your group and friendly players; Hostile over your target and anyone fighting you or your group. A static one is a plain box, up to 4 rows of 8 and 16 slots. A dynamic one is a band — icons centred over the head, a **Row cap** of 3, 5 or 7 to a row, growing upward, up to 16 icons. Each has a **Height** (px of room left for the game's nameplate) and a **Range** (hidden past that many metres). The game doesn't list who is around you, so a character gets its grid once something names it: your group, your target, someone you damage or heal, or whoever casts on a character already known. Preview mode shows an empty sample of each over your target.
- **Per-class grids.** Class tabs sit under the Grids toolbar: All, then the twelve classes in four groups (Guard Conq DT · PoM ToS BS · Barb Sin Ranger · Demo Necro HoX), each tab counting its own grids. A tab lists its own grids only, and a new grid joins the tab you add it on. In game a class builds its own grids and the All grids, so one profile can hold a layout for every class you play. A grid card's **Move…** menu has Move to and Copy to; a copy is named after the grid and the class. The tab you were on reopens with the app.
- **One Overlay size and Font for everything KazBars draws in game.** The Grids tab has an **Overlay** dropdown (10 to 24; 12 is labelled for 1080p, 16 for 1440p, 24 for 4K) and a **Font** dropdown: Arial, Verdana (the game's own), GG and nine open fonts — Roboto, Inter, Noto Sans, Source Sans 3, PT Sans, Fira Sans, Barlow Semi Condensed, Exo 2 and Alegreya Sans. Every grid, and the Stopwatch, Inspect panel, Cast timer, Console and Onslaught tracker, has its own **Size** slider — a percent of the Overlay size, 50 to 200 — with a hint beside it of what that comes to (48 px icons, 12 pt text). The KazBars Preview panel follows the Overlay size itself. Both dropdowns are saved in the profile.
- **Timers: Digits, Bar or Off.** Each grid card's **Timers** dropdown replaces the old on/off. Digits is the seconds left under each icon, as before. Bar is a line inside the icon's bottom edge that shrinks as the time runs out. Off shows none. Y Offset moves digits only, so it greys out for Bar and Off. Overhead grids start on Bar; a grid you had with timers off stays off.
- **Applied by me.** A switch on each grid card: the grid shows an effect only while you are the one who applied it.
- **Pick the Inspect panel's stat rows, and a PvP tab.** Extras ▸ Inspect panel… has a **Stat rows** card: 112 rows in seven groups — Summary, Vitals, Attributes, Offensive, Defensive, PvP Stats and PvP Combat. **Default** shows the twelve Summary rows (so a panel you haven't touched shows those until you choose more); **Custom list** shows the rows you pick, in your own order within each section. Search matches a name, a group or a stat ID, a Group filter narrows the Available list, and picking one row shows what it reads. The dialog warns when your rows would run taller than a 1080p game screen at your size. Player targets get a **PvP tab** in game — PvP combat stats, protections, bonus spell damage and their K/D record.
- **Console** (Extras ▸ Console…) — the buff-discovery console is its own extra, with a **Buffs** tab (every buff and debuff on you and your target, with its buff ID) and a **Casts** tab (your and your target's casts as they end: finished, interrupted or lost, and how long they took). Tick the tabs you want and order them; a tab you leave out costs nothing, and a console with no tab isn't built. It has its own Size slider and a card beside the other extras. Off by default.
- **Onslaught tracker** (Extras ▸ Onslaught tracker…) — an in-game extra for the Onslaught instances, Skull Gate and Kuthchemes; it only runs inside those two. The board lists the bosses, skulls and minis in view with their health; the wave timer counts to the next spawn; Hylda HP follows Valkyrie Hylda, the ally you defend in Skull Gate, with alerts at 50 % and 25 %; Spawn alerts banner a watched boss, skull or mini when it spawns; AoE warnings flag Thugra Khotan's AoE helpers in Kuthchemes. It always watches 27 mobs from its catalog, can list the Kuthchemes camp beasts on the board (never alerted), and watches up to 20 names of your own as minis — spelled exactly as in game, with the reason shown as you type if a name is refused. Choose which of the five displays you want, the alert time (3 to 15 s) and the Size; place the board and the alerts in preview mode. Has its own card. Off by default.
- **A Font for Damage Numbers.** Extras ▸ Damage number mod… has a Style card with a **Font**: Hyborian, the game's own, is the default and leaves the stock file as it is. Nine heavy display faces — GG Comic, Bangers, Lilita One, Rubik Black, Bungee, Changa One, Cinzel Decorative Black, Alfa Slab One and Bevan — are built into the mod on your next Build & Install. A face that runs small is drawn larger to read as big as Hyborian, and the dialog shows by how much.
- **Overhead anchor** (Game ▸ Game resolution…) — **Right** and **Up** fields for where overhead grids sit against a character's head. It depends on your game's field of view, so it's kept on this PC, not in the profile; a change needs a Build & Install.
- **Share grids.** A grid card's **Move…** menu now ends with **Export grid…**, which saves that grid to a `<grid name>.kazbars-grids.yml` file. **File ▸ Import grid…**, right under Import profile…, adds a grids file's grids to the class tab the Grids tab is showing and switches to the Grids tab so you see them; the toast reads like "Imported 2 grids into Dark Templar — renamed ‘Buffs’ to ‘Buffs_2’ (name taken) — 1 custom buff added". A grid whose name is taken gets `_2` added. Custom buffs travel inside the file and are added to your database on import. Picking a grids file in Import profile… points you to Import grid… instead.
- **Reset profile to defaults…** (File menu) asks first, then replaces your profile with the starter layout, sized for your game resolution. Revert to session start undoes it.

### Changed

- **KazBars is now licensed under the GNU GPL v2 or later.** Nothing changes in how you use it — anyone may copy, modify, and share it under the same terms. Third-party parts keep their own terms; see [THIRD_PARTY.md](../THIRD_PARTY.md).
- **Builds no longer run a separate compiler program.** KazBars compiles your grids itself instead of shelling out to a bundled `mtasc.exe`, which is gone from the download. Build & Install works exactly the same otherwise.
- **The Database tab has no Save button — buff edits save themselves.** Every add, edit, delete, import and category rename is written to disk the moment you confirm it, so an interrupted session can't lose buff work.
- **First launch fills in your game folder when there's only one.** When exactly one Age of Conan install is found on the PC, the path is already in the box with "Load Defaults" ready to press. Two or more, or none, and nothing is assumed — a line under the buttons says what's missing. "Set up later" at the bottom now reads as the link it always was.
- **The four extras cards moved to the bottom of the Grids tab**, next to Build & Install, so the grid list starts at the top of the page. Toasts stack above the cards, clear of their switches.
- **Clicking an extras card now opens its settings.** The four cards at the bottom of the Grids tab used to be one big on/off switch. Now the card and the switch do different jobs: click anywhere on the card — title, status line, the box itself — and that feature's dialog opens, the same one Extras ▸ Cast timer…, Damage number mod…, Inspect panel… and Stopwatch… open. The round switch on the right still decides whether the feature ships in your next Build & Install.
- **Grid cards are calmer.** A grid's player/target colour is now a stripe down the card's left edge instead of a full coloured outline.
- **Sizes are a percent of one Overlay size.** Icon, timer and stack sizes, and the text size of every panel, now come from the Overlay size and each item's Size slider instead of separate pixel and point fields. Your existing profiles convert on load and keep their in-game sizes — exactly for the shipped icon sizes, within one 5 % step for a size you tuned. A panel that followed the shared panel size now follows the Overlay size. First launch picks the Overlay size for your resolution (12 up to 1080p, 16 up to 1440p, 24 above).
- **Changing the game resolution no longer rescales anything.** Positions were already fractions of the screen; sizes are now a percent of the Overlay size, so Game ▸ Game resolution… leaves the profile alone. On a bigger screen, pick a bigger Overlay size on the Grids tab. Rebuild to apply, as before.
- **A grid holds up to 64 slots on its own — there is no total across grids.** The 64-slot cap that counted every grid together is gone, because in game a slot that shows nothing costs nothing. Add Grid no longer counts down "available slots"; each grid is limited by its own shape (64 slots, 16 for an overhead grid), and Build & Install stops only for a grid over its own limit.
- **A grid that tracks nothing is left out of the build.** A dynamic grid with no Tracked Buffs, or a static grid with nothing in its slots, isn't built empty. It stays in your profile and ships once you give it something.
- **Dragging in preview mode no longer prints live X/Y, and panels keep their spot when the screen resizes.** The readout is gone from grids, the Cast timer and every panel — the X/Y fields in the app only seed a first-ever session, and nothing types a position in. The Stopwatch, Inspect panel and Console now seat where you left them on whatever size the screen turns out to be, so a saved spot isn't clamped into place while the screen still reads a smaller size at login.
- **A grid card shows the grid, and you pick buffs on it.** Every card now draws its grid to scale, with icons at two-thirds of their in-game size. A grid too big for the card steps down, and the title line says so ("shown at 1:2"). A line under the preview names the buffs the grid holds ("… + N more"), or says what to click when it holds nothing. The **Tracked Buffs...** button and the Slot Assignment window are gone. Click a dynamic grid's preview to choose its buffs. On a static grid, click a slot; the picker is titled "Slot 3 Buffs" and walks the grid's slots itself: numbered chips under the header (the current slot filled in the grid's colour, assigned slots outlined), ◀ ▶ beside Cancel, and Ctrl+PageUp / Ctrl+PageDown anywhere in the window. Moving to another slot saves the slot you leave, Done saves the current one, and Cancel drops only the current slot's unsaved change. The status line says "All N slots are assigned." once every slot holds a buff. Right-click a slot, or arrow to it and press Delete, to clear it; a toast offers "click to undo" for 8 seconds. Tab reaches the preview, arrows move between slots and Enter opens. Cards no longer expand: a ⚙ cog just right of the preview, at its top, opens "Grid Settings — <name>", centred on the KazBars window. The title line's right side holds **Move…**, Enabled and ×. Grid Settings' labels line up in one column, and it has up to four cards: Grid (Name; Rows × Cols, or an overhead dynamic grid's Row cap and Max slots; Position X / Y for screen grids), Icons (Size, Gap, Timers with Y offset greyed unless Digits, Flash with Under greyed while off), Overhead (Height, Range, Over me on friendly grids) and Sorting (Fill, Sort, Order on dynamic grids). Every change, including dragging Size, shows on the card's preview at once; Cancel (or Esc) puts it back, Apply (or Enter) saves, and Enter on the Cancel button cancels. A name another grid already uses blocks Apply, with a hint under the Name field. **Applied by me** is a round toggle on the card's title line, right after the mode note, no longer in Grid Settings; it greys with the card when the grid is disabled. The card's summary now says only the mode ("dynamic" or "static"), plus " · shown at 1:2" when the preview is drawn smaller. Hovering a dynamic grid's preview lights the whole grid. The preset cards on an empty Grids tab draw their shapes the same way.
- **The extras row has six cards.** Console and Onslaught tracker join Cast timer, Damage numbers, Inspect panel and Stopwatch, in the same order as the Extras menu. The card opens its dialog; the switch decides whether it ships. Flip the switch of an extra with nothing to build — a console with no tab, an Onslaught tracker with no display ticked — and the toast says so.
- **Settings dialogs open centred on KazBars every time.** Cast timer, Console, Damage numbers, Damage number colors, Inspect panel, Stopwatch, Onslaught tracker, Game resolution, Grid Settings, Default buff bars and Backup & Restore no longer reopen where you last dragged them. The main window, Deeps, the Live Tracker, Add Grid and the buff picker still remember their spot.
- **One profile instead of a profile list.** The whole app uses one profile, `userdata/profile.yml`, and it autosaves as before (Ctrl+S still saves now). The File menu is now Export profile…, Import profile…, Import grid…, Reset profile to defaults…, Revert to session start, Open data folder, Exit. The window title bar reads KazBars, with no profile name, and Reset profile to defaults… keeps your profile's name.
- **Profile files are YAML.** Export profile… writes `<name>.kazbars.yml`, including any custom buffs the profile uses. Import profile… opens a `.kazbars.yml`, a `.kazbars.json` export from 3.1, or a profile file copied from a 3.1 `userdata/profiles` folder; the imported profile replaces the open one and its custom buffs are added to your database. Revert to session start brings back the profile you had before an Import or a Reset. The stock default profile is a YAML file too; the buff database is unchanged.
- **First launch over an older install adopts your active profile.** KazBars takes the profile you were using as your profile and a toast says which. Your other old profiles stay untouched in `userdata/profiles`, and Import profile… opens any of them.
- **A damaged `profile.yml` falls back to your last session's copy.** The damaged file is kept as `profile.damaged.yml`, and a toast says so.
- **Backup & restore carries your profile.** Restoring a backup made by 3.1 brings back its profiles, and KazBars adopts the one that was active.

### Removed

- **The per-grid Icon size, Stack Font and timer Font spinboxes.** One **Size** slider per grid sets icon, timer and stack size together, as a percent of the Overlay size. The Cast timer's Font size spinbox is a Size slider the same way.
- **The shared panel text size.** Extras ▸ Inspect panel… no longer holds a Text size for all four in-game panels, and the Stopwatch and Inspect panel dialogs lose "Use the shared size for this panel" and their own-size spinboxes. Each panel has a Size slider; the preview control panel follows the Overlay size.
- **The buff-discovery console switch in the Inspect panel dialog.** The console is Extras ▸ Console… now. A console you had switched on stays off until you tick it there.
- **The Inspect panel's "Show the PvP section" and "Track slotted perks" switches.** PvP is a tab in game, and the Perks row always shows on a player target.
- **New profile, New from template, Duplicate profile, Rename profile… and Delete profile…, and the profile list in the File menu.** Ctrl+N does nothing now.
- **Open profiles folder.** File ▸ Open data folder opens the userdata folder instead.

### Fixed

- **Esc closes more windows.** It closes the Game Resolution dialog, the settings backup dialog and the welcome dialog (the same as "Set up later" — a game folder you typed is still kept), and hides the Deeps and Ethram-Fal Live Tracker windows without stopping their monitoring. Enter applies in the resolution dialog.
- **Grids can be reordered from the keyboard.** Tab to a grid card's ☰ handle, then Alt+↑ / Alt+↓ moves it one place.
- **Two rough edges in the Add Grid wizard.** The "Exceeds available slots" warning was too dark to read against its background, and an empty band sat below the buttons.
- **The Deeps and Ethram-Fal Live Tracker windows no longer cut off their last line.** Both size themselves to their content as they open, so a status line that wrapped to two lines — a long combat-log filename in Deeps, a long game folder path in the tracker — used to push the bottom row off the edge, with no way to resize or scroll to it. They now resize when the status line changes.
- **Extras cards now light up the part you're about to click.** Hover the card body — title, status line, the box — and its border lights up to show it opens that feature's settings; hover the switch instead and only the switch gets a border, leaving the card at rest. Flipping the switch while your mouse was still over the card body used to leave the wrong border lit until you moved away and back — fixed. Clicking the card body to open its settings dialog had the same problem in reverse: the border could stay lit even after you closed the dialog and moved your mouse well away, because the dialog takes over the mouse while it's up and the card never learned it had left — now the highlight clears the moment you click to open the dialog. The switch also shows a hand cursor now, matching the rest of the card, and the card's tooltip reaches you from anywhere on the card — title, status line, edge — not just a thin border strip.
- **Some warning and error text was too dark to read against its background.** Two sentences in the in-app guide, the grid slot counter once you're past 95% of your budget, the ✕ "failed" icon on the Build & Install results screen (both places it shows), and the small × that deletes a grid card on hover or press — all now use a lighter, readable red.
- **Settings dialogs confirm on Enter from anywhere, and open ready to use.** The Cast Timer, Damage Numbers, Damage Number Colors, Inspect Panel and Stopwatch dialogs, plus the buff-edit dialog in the Database tab, now confirm on Enter no matter where your cursor is in the dialog — not just when a button already has focus. Each one also opens with something sensibly focused, so keyboard use works right away instead of needing a first click.
- **First launch stopped listing the folder it already filled in.** When exactly one Age of Conan install is found, the path goes straight into the box — but that same path also still showed under "Found on this PC:" right below it. That list now hides once the box already holds one of its paths, and comes back if you clear the box or type or pick something else.
- **The game folder path in the bottom bar was shortening too eagerly.** A plain `C:\Program Files (x86)\Age of Conan\...` install used to collapse straight down to `C:\...\Age of Conan` — drive letter and final folder only. It now shows the whole path for anything up to a typical Steam-under-Program-Files depth (checked against the app's minimum window width, so it's guaranteed to fit) — a normal install shows in full now, and only a genuinely deep, unusual folder still shortens. The shortened form also used to slip in a stray double backslash right after the drive letter (`C:\\...\Age of Conan`); it's a single backslash now, like the rest of the path.
- **Cast Timer's dialog header matches the others now.** Extras ▸ Cast timer… had a dusty-rose header stripe while Damage Numbers, Inspect Panel and Stopwatch all shared the same blue — a deliberate choice once, but it read as inconsistent. It's blue now too.

## [3.1.1] — 2026-08-31

### Changed

- **KazBars has its own icon.** A 3×3 buff grid with the top row lit in the app's blue, in the title bar, the taskbar, Alt-Tab and on `KazBars.exe` itself — replacing the generic placeholder and the ttkbootstrap logo.

## [3.1.0] — 2026-08-30

### Added

- **KazBars updates itself.** When a newer release is out, the launch notice reads "KazBars vX is available — click to install": one click downloads it in the background, a second click restarts KazBars into the new version. Your profiles, settings, custom buffs and buff-database updates stay exactly where they are — no re-download, no first-time setup, no old folder to delete. If a download fails, nothing changes and the notice opens the release page instead. Installs older than this release still need one manual update: extract the new zip over your existing KazBars folder.

### Changed

- **One check for everything.** Updates ▸ Check for updates now looks for a new KazBars release first and, when you're already current, for buff-database updates — replacing the two separate menu items. The About window's check now starts the install directly.

## [3.0.1] — 2026-08-30

### Changed

- **Buff-database updates now need KazBars 3.0.0.** The shipped starter profile moved to the document format, so the content feed that carries it is stamped for 3.0.0 and up; a 2.2.2 install sees "New buffs are available — update KazBars to get them" and keeps the catalog it has. New from template reads one starter — the shipped one, or the content feed's newer copy when there is one.

### Fixed

- **Big layouts build again.** Build & Install used to stop with "MTASC compilation failed: Class KazBarsData excess 32K bytecode limit" once a layout tracked roughly 600 or more distinct buffs across its grids — the stock starter profile plus one more grid of about 50 fresh buffs was enough. All of a layout's buff data sat in one compiled class, and the game's compiler caps each class at 32 KB; it now splits across as many as the layout needs, so any layout within the 64-slot cap builds no matter how large the buff database grows. Nothing changes in-game.

## [3.0.0] — 2026-08-29

### Added
- **Your in-game layout survives relogs and restarts — for everyone.** Build & Install now permanently registers KazBars with the game: two small, clearly marked blocks in the game's own config XMLs (pristine `.bak` backups beside them) plus `IgnorePatcher.enable`, the engine's own patcher-skip flag, so launching the game executable directly loads KazBars every time. Drag grids and panels in preview mode (Ctrl+Shift+Alt) and the game remembers positions, collapse states, and the control panel's checkboxes across relogs and full restarts — the same way it remembers every other game window. The X/Y fields in the app are now just the defaults a first-ever session starts from. The Aoc.exe question is gone: no launcher-bypass detection, no two sets of instructions — one install mode for every user. The game *and* the patcher must be closed for the first build only; after that, rebuild anytime and apply with `/reloadui`. Uninstall restores every changed game file byte-for-byte.
- **Repair game install** (Game ▸ Repair game install…) — the patch-day recovery. If the game ever updates, run the official patcher once, then Repair: it re-registers KazBars, restores your saved positions from a safety snapshot the app keeps fresh, and reinstalls the Damage Numbers mod if you use one. A startup health check notices when the game no longer loads KazBars and offers the repair in one click.
- **Desktop shortcut for direct launch.** After your first successful build, KazBars offers to create a desktop shortcut to the game executable (DX10 or DX9), so the patcher-skipping launch is one double-click. Also available anytime via Game ▸ Create game desktop shortcut….
- **The KazBars Preview panel.** Preview mode's control panel is now titled **KazBars Preview**, with one checkbox per grid and in-game extra grouped under three clickable headers — Player Grids, Target Grids and Tools (Cast Timer, Inspect Panel, Buff Console, Stopwatch). Untick a box to hide that item while you position things; click a header to toggle its whole group at once, and a group with nothing in it doesn't appear. Each box is a persistent master switch: unchecked means off, and it stays off after a relog until you check it again. A dim footer carries the essentials for a first-time visitor: the keybind that exits preview, the Build & Install + `/reloadui` recipe for seeing app-side changes in game, and the app version with the maintainer credit.
- **Extras-only builds.** Build & Install no longer requires a grid: a layout with no grids but an enabled extra (Stopwatch, Inspect panel, Cast timer, Damage Numbers) now builds.
- **Target inspect panel** (Extras ▸ Inspect panel…) — an optional in-game overlay that shows a combat sheet for whatever you have targeted, styled after the game's own inspect window: armor and protection with their mitigation percentages, crit and critigation, heal rating with the Celestial Gaze range it buys, bonus spell damage, combat rating — and, on player targets only, the PvP section with its own bonus spell damage and combat rating (mobs and bosses show the PvE sheet). The title line identifies the target — name, class, and level/PvP level, dropping whatever a target doesn't have. A Sections group in the dialog offers two toggles, both on by default and applied on your next Build & Install: **Show the PvP section** governs the PvP block (on by default, so nothing changes unless you untick it), and **Track slotted perks** adds a Perks row at the bottom of the panel on player targets — up to six of the game's own buff icons for the AA perks detected on the target. The row mirrors the game's own perk bar — six boxes in three colour-coded pairs, two blue for General, two red for Archetype, two grey for Class — so a perk always appears in its own pair and the colour tells you what kind it is. Three of every class's seven perks take both class slots, and the panel paints those across both Class boxes just as the game does, so they read as two identical icons rather than a gap. A box is empty when the target hasn't filled that slot, or when an active perk isn't currently running — only passive perks and active ones that are running show. A perk you were given by a groupmate can show as well, and empty slots draw as empty boxes. Turn either off and the built overlay drops that section entirely. Turn it on in the new dialog and it ships with your next Build & Install; the position and font size you pick are baked in, and the whole panel scales with the font size. Dragging its name strip shows live coordinates — type them into the dialog to make a spot permanent; the − button folds the panel down to that strip, and the game remembers both the drag and the folded state across relogs. Off by default; when off, the built overlay carries no inspect-panel code at all.
- **Seal of Yog (Crit) and Seal of Yog (Mana)** — the Dark Templar stacking buffs join the catalog, every stack rank covered. (Already delivered to existing installs as a content update; see `database-changelog.md`.)
- **Every combat number now gets a direction, not just a color** (Extras ▸ Damage number colors…). Each of the ~35 sources gains a **Rising** / **Dropping** / **Zig-zag** dropdown beside its swatch — float it above the head, drop it into the fixed column, or add it to the zig-zag stack — named to match the Damage Numbers panel's position cards. A **Number directions** card at the top carries two shortcuts that move a whole group at once: **Group my resource numbers** sends your own mana and stamina losses down to the fixed column where your gains already land, and **Send incoming numbers to the fixed column** does the same for everything that hits you. Both simply set the per-source dropdowns, so you can fine-tune any row afterwards — the checkbox unticks once its group is mixed. "Reset to game default" now restores the stock colour *and* direction, per row or for the whole panel.
- **One text size for all four in-game panels** (Extras ▸ Inspect panel… ▸ Text size). The stopwatch, inspect panel, buff-discovery console and preview control panel now share a single 8–48 size, baked in at build time — each panel scales as one piece, collapsed bars included, so they keep matching at any size. The control sits in the Inspect panel dialog for the same reason that dialog already holds the console's build toggle: the console and the control panel have no dialog of their own. Either of the other two can still go its own way — untick **Use the shared size for this panel** in the Stopwatch or Inspect panel dialog and its own spinbox (now labelled **Stopwatch only** / **Inspect panel only**) takes over. Nothing you already set moves: the shared size starts from whatever your inspect panel was set to, and a stopwatch that disagreed with it keeps its number as an override, so both panels render exactly as they did before. What does change is the console and the control panel — previously frozen at the size-12 equivalents, with no setting at all — which pick up the shared size on your next Build & Install. The control panel is the one panel whose size grows with content, a row per grid, so at large sizes with many grids it quietly steps its own text down until it fits the screen.
- **Toggle cards for the four in-game extras, right on the main screen.** A row above the grid list — Cast timer, Damage numbers, Inspect panel, Stopwatch, in the same order as the Extras menu — shows what ships in your next Build & Install: a card lights up green with "In next build" when its feature is on, and reads a dimmed "Off" when it isn't. Click anywhere on a card (or its switch) to flip the feature; it changes the exact same setting the feature's dialog saves. The row is there even before you've added a grid, and configuring each feature stays where it was — its dialog in the Extras menu.
- **20 more wrack and torment boss debuffs join the catalog**, completing the dispel set on several raid encounters that previously only had their ruin half tracked. (Already delivered to existing installs as a content update; see `database-changelog.md`.)
- **The generic Wrack and Torment lines now catch the game's later re-issued copies.** Physical, Elemental and Spiritual Wrack carry the spell IDs the game issues for newer content as aliases, and three new Torment (Generic) entries catch every same-named copy that falls outside the tracked 1-5 stack ranks. Sodabeh's Wrack (Ardashir Fort) joins the catalog, Nature's Wrath now tracks every stack instead of only the tenth, and Crushed Armor Wrack is credited to Dai Gang. Two new categories tidy the list: `#Dungeons` — dungeon debuffs, now named by the instance you're in rather than the boss — and `#World Bosses`. The starter template's and stock default profile's whitelists were refreshed to match, bringing in the Torment (Generic) entries and more class buffs. (Already delivered to existing installs as a content update; see `database-changelog.md`.)
- **24 new player crowd-control entries, cast and confirmed in game rather than assumed from tooltips.** Covers every class's `#Crowd Control` list, with corrected spell IDs, added ranks, and effects renamed to say what they actually do (fear-and-slow instead of just fear, silence-and-stun instead of just silence). (Already delivered to existing installs as a content update; see `database-changelog.md`.)
- **Ruin-line boss debuffs expanded and cleaned up** — new entries confirmed in game, roughly a dozen given their applying boss as a name suffix, four unconfirmed ones withdrawn pending verification, and the three generic Ruin debuffs gained the alias spell IDs the game issues for later content. (Already delivered to existing installs as a content update; see `database-changelog.md`.)

### Changed
- **About shows the version it's running.** The bottom line now reads `v3.0.0 · MIT License`. The Discord link is gone from the dialog for now — the invite still lives in the README.
- **Profiles are a managed library now — no more Open/Save dialogs.** The File menu lists every profile you have; click one to switch, or use New profile, New from template, Duplicate profile, Rename profile…, Delete profile… and Revert to session start to manage them. There's no Save button and no dirty state to lose: every edit autosaves to the profile's own file about a second after you stop editing, and Build & Install flushes first so what's installed always matches what's saved. A quiet status line under the extras cards ("In game: <profile>") turns orange when the installed build no longer matches the loaded profile — a different profile installed, an edit since the last build, or a resolution change. File ▸ Export profile… and File ▸ Import profile… replace the old copy-paste string: Export writes one self-contained `.kazbars.json` file with any custom buffs the profile references embedded inside it, so the importer's own database doesn't need to know them first. Grid, Stopwatch, Inspect panel and Cast timer positions travel as fractions of your resolution now, so a shared profile lands proportionally on any screen instead of at the fixed pixel spot it was authored for. Nearly everything else a build depends on lives in the profile too — grids, Stopwatch, Inspect panel, Cast timer and Damage Numbers — and so do Deeps and the Ethram-Fal Live Tracker's live settings, plus any Default Buff Bars or Damage Number Colors overrides you've made (only the fields you've actually changed; everything else still defers to the game). Two things stay machine-local: the shared panel text size and the buff-discovery console toggle. A buff reference an import or a database change can't resolve no longer disappears silently: it shows greyed out as "unknown buff" in Tracked Buffs, and the Build & Install summary counts how many were excluded. **Profiles made with 2.2.2 or earlier don't appear in the new library — the format changed, so old files are left on disk untouched but invisible.** Recreate your layout with File ▸ New from template; this is why this release is a major version bump rather than a patch.
- **The quick-start tip guide only shows once.** After your first build, the four-step callout on the main screen retires for good — it won't reappear on a later launch or after loading a different profile.
- **Applying a rebuild is `/reloadui` alone.** The `/reloadgrids` and `/unloadgrids` chat scripts are gone — they existed to work around positions not persisting on the standard launcher, and nothing needs them now.
- **The in-app guide is now a knowledge base.** Help gained sections it never had — **Profiles** (what a profile is, which one opens on launch, how to export and import one as a file), **Updates** (what a buff-database update can and can't touch, and the fact that KazBars never updates itself), **Launching the Game**, **After a Game Patch** and **Removing KazBars**. The buff-discovery console now has one home of its own, **Finding Buff IDs**, instead of being explained twice; the rules every extra follows are stated once in **How Extras Ship**, so each feature's own section is just that feature. Sections link to each other now — click a link to jump straight there.
- **Damage Numbers no longer needs the Aoc.exe launcher bypass.** With the patcher skipped on every launch, the mod's replaced file stays put. If the game ever patches, run the patcher once, then Build & Install to put the mod back.
- **The buff console's open state is remembered — the Keep Open pin is retired.** Whether you left the console open, where it sits, and whether it's folded all persist across relogs, like everything else in the overlay.
- **Damage number colors now apply the moment you hit Apply — no build, no toggle.** The color editor (Extras ▸ Damage number colors…) works like the Default Buff Bars editor: pick your colors, click Apply, and type `/reloadui` in-game to see them. It edits the game's `TextColors.xml` directly instead of waiting for a Build & Install, and it no longer depends on the Damage Numbers mod being switched on — colors are the game's own feature, so they stand on their own. Edits land in your `Customized` skin folder (created for you if needed) and touch only what you set, so a game update can't wipe them. "Reset to game default" pulls the original color straight from the game's stock files. Colors you set now stay put when you turn the Damage Numbers mod off or uninstall — use Reset if you want them back to stock.
- **Your number directions are yours now.** The Damage Numbers mod, Build & Install and Uninstall no longer touch `TextColors.xml` at all — colours and directions both belong to the Damage number colors editor, which writes them the moment you hit Apply. Turning the mod off, rebuilding, or uninstalling no longer reverts a direction you chose; "Reset to game default" is how you get stock back, exactly as it already worked for colours.
- **Two Damage Numbers toggles now say what they actually do.** "Separate resources into Column B" is now **Split signed numbers into Column B**: of the numbers already dropping into the fixed column, plain damage stays in Column A and the signed ones — heals, stamina, mana — move to Column B. "Group my resource numbers" is now **Keep enemy drains overhead**: mana and stamina you drain from enemies keep floating over them even when your own resource numbers are set to Dropping. Both are settings baked into the mod; *which* sources drop into the fixed column is now picked per source in Damage number colors, where the "Group my resource numbers" shortcut moved.
- **The Damage Numbers panel waits for Apply.** Settings no longer save as you drag a slider: **Apply** saves and closes, and Cancel, Escape or the X discard everything you changed. Loading a preset stages it the same way.
- **The Extras menu is split into two groups** — the editors that write your game files (Damage number colors, Default buff bars), applied with `/reloadui`, and the ones that ship with your next Build & Install (Cast timer, Damage number mod, Inspect panel, Stopwatch). Each group is a flat, alphabetized list with no caption, split by a single separator; the in-app guide's How Extras Ship section explains what the split means. "In-game stopwatch…" is now **Stopwatch…** and "Target inspect panel…" is now **Inspect panel…**. Both dialogs now carry those names in their title bar and header too, and the in-app guide follows.
- **The Cast Timer moved out of the Grids panel into its own dialog** (Extras ▸ Cast timer…). The strip that used to sit pinned above the grid list is gone; everything it carried is in the dialog, with the same Apply flow as the Stopwatch and Inspect panel: one **Include the cast timer in builds** master toggle, Player and Target positions with their X/Y, and the text settings — Bold, font size (8–48), what the timer shows (Elapsed / Total / Both) and its colour, with the live sample preview beside them. Apply saves and closes; Cancel, Escape and the X discard. Nothing reaches the game until your next Build & Install, exactly as before. Its settings live in the profile alongside the Stopwatch and Inspect panel, so a shared profile carries its positions too, as fractions of your screen.
- **The buff-discovery console toggle moved into the Inspect panel dialog** (Extras ▸ Inspect panel… ▸ Console), saved with that dialog's Apply. It's the other in-game inspection tool, so both switch on from one place.
- **The in-game stopwatch and buff console match the inspect panel.** Both were re-skinned to the same warm near-black plate, bronze frame, Conan-orange title and square corners, with the stopwatch's time reading green, orange or grey by state. The collapsed stopwatch bar can be clicked to open the panel again, and the console remembers where you dragged it and can't be pushed off screen. The stopwatch and inspect dialogs also reopen where you left them, and Escape closes them.
- **The buff-discovery console collapses like the rest of the overlay.** A − button on its title line folds the console down to a small labelled bar — the same bar the stopwatch and inspect panel collapse to — and clicking that bar opens it again. Whether you left it open or collapsed is remembered along with where you dragged it, and both restore across a relog.
- **The three in-game panels now share one title bar.** Stopwatch, inspect panel and buff console all read the same way: title on the left in Conan orange, the − / + button in the same corner, the same hairline under the title band. The collapsed bars match too — the stopwatch's label no longer renders bigger than the other two, and its + now sits exactly where theirs do. Dragging any of the three shows the live x, y readout in the same right-hand spot.
- **The buff console shrinks to what you're logging.** Untick a side and its column disappears — the panel narrows to a single column instead of keeping an empty half. Untick both and it drops to a small strip with just the toggles. The header ticks stay visible either way, so you can always bring a side back, and nothing is lost in the resize: collected IDs, position and fold state all carry over.
- **The Stopwatch, Inspect panel and Cast timer dialogs now read like the Damage Numbers panel.** Same shape top to bottom: a tip line under the header, the master include-in-builds toggle at the top, the settings grouped into titled cards, and Apply / Cancel along the bottom. Untick the master toggle and everything it governs greys out — controls, labels, even the cast timer's colour sample — so a switched-off feature looks off at a glance. In the Inspect panel dialog, the shared text size and the console toggle stay live when the panel itself is off, because they're their own settings that just live in that dialog.
- **The reset button in Damage number colors sits at the end of its row.** Each source now reads label, direction, swatch, then ↺ — the reset comes after both of the things it resets instead of sitting between them.

### Fixed
- **Changing the game resolution now resizes your grids' icons and fonts too.** They step to the new resolution's sizes by the same ratio the starter template uses, so a grid you never touched lands on the stock size and a grid you tuned keeps its tuning; positions were already resolution-independent.
- **Tooltips no longer get stuck on screen.** Removing a control while its tooltip was showing — deleting a grid card, for example — could leave the tooltip floating over the app. Tooltips now disappear with their control.
- **Buff console entries read in the console's own type again.** Every logged buff name and ID was falling back to a mismatched serif font; entries now render in the same face as the rest of the panel — the buff name in parchment grey, `ID: nnnn` in green.
- **Grid names with accented or non-Latin characters (é, ü, 日本語…) no longer fail to compile.** The build's internal identifier for each grid now also replaces any character MTASC's compiler wouldn't accept, instead of assuming any letter was safe just because it wasn't ASCII punctuation.
- **Restoring settings no longer risks losing unsaved profile edits.** Game ▸ Backup & restore game settings… now saves your current profile before restoring over it, and stops with a clear warning instead of proceeding if that save fails.
- **Uninstall now checks whether the game is actually running before it starts,** the same way Repair game install and Build & Install already did — close it first is enforced instead of risking a half-applied uninstall. Uninstall and Repair now share the same check.
- **A safety backup no longer resurrects a game config file you deleted on purpose.** Repair and uninstall used to restore `Customized/Modules.xml` from its own backup even when you'd removed the file yourself; a missing file is now left missing.
- **Build & Install re-checks whether KazBars is actually registered with the game, not just whether you've ever built before.** If a game patch quietly wiped the registration, the close-the-game prompt now reappears instead of getting skipped.
- **A buff-database content update now takes effect the moment it's applied — no restart needed.** Applying or reverting an update used to land on disk but stay invisible in the app until you relaunched. An app update that raises the minimum content version now also falls back to its own bundled catalog instead of continuing to use content an older KazBars had already downloaded.
- **KazBars's cleanup of `Scripts/auto_login` no longer changes the file's line endings** — anything else you keep in that script is left exactly as it was.
- **A malformed or hand-edited grid config value no longer crashes a build.** A bare, non-list buff reference in a grid's slot assignments is now handled the same way a list of references already was.
- **Switching profiles or closing the app when a save fails now warns you and keeps you on the unsaved profile,** instead of silently discarding your changes and swapping in a fresh store.
- **The profile list no longer crashes if a profile file disappears mid-listing** (an antivirus scan, a sync client, and the like) — listing and switching profiles is more robust to files coming and going out from under it.
- **In-game dragged positions reset once on this upgrade.** Saved spots used to be keyed by a grid's position in the list, so reordering or deleting a grid could hand its saved spot to the wrong one. Positions are now keyed to the grid itself — accurate from here on, but every dragged position resets this one time; reposition once in preview mode and it holds normally after that.
- **Preview mode keeps every overlay on top, in bounds and readable.** A tall panel (the inspect panel at larger font sizes on 1080p) could get dragged off-screen with nothing left to grab it back with; retargeting mid-preview could resize the inspect panel out from under its own label; a two-column grid's live X/Y readout could clip a digit; and a buff that changed category mid-fight could draw over the frame around it instead of at its assigned depth. All four are fixed.
- **The inspect panel clears the moment you drop or swap target in preview mode**, instead of leaving the previous target's numbers visible underneath the overlay until something else happens to repaint it.
- **A toast's undo now undoes the right action, and a stopped overlay no longer leaves a ghost on screen.** Deleting a run of grids back-to-back could coalesce their toasts but keep only the first one's undo, so clicking undid an earlier delete instead of the latest; toggling the Deeps or Ethram-Fal overlay's visibility or lock while it was stopped could leave a stale copy on screen. Importing buffs also now checks every ID for a collision, not just the first one, so a shared secondary ID can't silently re-home an existing entry.
- **Changing your game folder repeatedly no longer stacks tooltips.** Re-hovering the Game: label after a change used to show the old and new folder paths overlapping instead of just the current one.
- **Deeps and the Ethram-Fal tracker no longer double-count after a quick Stop → Start.** The old worker thread could stay alive alongside the new one for a moment, double-firing readings and boss-timer triggers.
- **Update checks now read a suffixed release tag correctly** (e.g. `2.3.0-rc1`) instead of silently treating it as already up to date.
- **Buff-bar and damage-color edits now write to the game's XML atomically** — a temp file plus rename, so an interrupted write can't leave the file half-written.
- **The inspect panel now shows percentages for a target at any level, not just level 80.** It used to suppress every percent decode — mitigation, crit, heal range, and the rest — for anything off level 80, showing the raw rating alone instead. In-game measurement confirmed the underlying math (the 36.6 rating divisor, the mitigation curve constants) holds across the full 1–86 range, including 81–86 mobs, so that restriction is gone.

## [2.2.0] — 2026-06-15

### Added
- **Damage Numbers** (Game ▸ Damage number Mod… / Damage number Colors…) — a new panel that tunes Age of Conan's floating combat-number overlay and installs a leaner, faster rewrite of it on your next Build & Install. The headline fix: damage numbers no longer shrink to nothing at range — a **Keep ranged numbers big** toggle holds the size of *ranged* hits (past ~15 real metres) without ever touching your close-up (melee) numbers, which stay exactly as the game draws them. Also tune shadow style (None / Fast / Real), pop-in and fade speed, and where numbers land (above the target, in fixed columns, or a zig-zag stack), with Default / Performance presets. A **Group my resource numbers** toggle routes your own mana/stamina losses into the same fixed column as your resource gains — so all your resource changes read in one place — while mana/stamina you drain from enemies still floats over them. A **Separate resources into Column B** toggle drops everything that lands on you (incoming damage, heals, mana/stamina) into fixed columns and splits it — damage in one column; heals, stamina and mana in the next — for a clean, stationary readout instead of numbers flying off your head. And a **Damage number colors** editor lets you recolor every combat-number source independently — incoming vs outgoing hits, crits, spells, combos, heals, mana/stamina — laid out self on the left, your target on the right. Off by default behind a single master toggle; your stock `DamageInfo.swf` is backed up once, so turning it off or uninstalling restores the original.
- **In-game stopwatch** (Game ▸ In-game stopwatch…) — an optional Start / Pause / Reset count-up timer that lives *inside* the game as part of the KazBars overlay, so it works in fullscreen and its buttons never steal focus from the game. Turn it on in the new dialog and it ships with your next Build & Install: a compact draggable panel showing `h:mm:ss`, with a − button that collapses it to just its title bar (which then shows the running time). Dragging the title bar shows live coordinates — type them into the dialog to make a spot permanent; Aoc.exe clients remember the position and collapsed state automatically. Off by default; when off, the built overlay carries no stopwatch code at all.

### Changed
- **Deeps "Alarm & Tints" is simpler to set.** The DPS-out alarm is now a slider over a 1000–4000/s band (instead of a typed value), and the four ΔHP-in survival-tint thresholds collapse into two presets — **Standard** (DPS / healers) and **Tank** (a wider symmetric band) — with a caption that restates the breakpoints. The default survival tints changed to match the **Standard** preset; the alarm default stays 2500/s. Your saved alarm value is kept and clamped into the slider's band.
- **Buff catalog reorganized.** The shipped buff database is sorted into clearer categories — a new **#Protections** group for group damage-mitigation buffs, plus plainer names (#Resistances → #Immunities, #Global → #General, #Group HoT → #Group Heals) and the raid tiers grouped under **#Raid T3…T6**. Display grouping only — no spell IDs changed, so existing grids and profiles are unaffected. (Shipped as a content update; see `database-changelog.md`.)
- **Refreshed default profile.** The out-of-the-box grids were redesigned and renamed for clarity — **My Buffs**, **Raid Debuffs**, **Target Buffs**, **Target Debuffs** — authored against a 1440p reference that scales to any resolution.
- **Internal** (no user-facing change) — `ui_widgets.py` split into focused modules: `ui_headers.py` (dialog/app headers), `ui_forms.py` (form fields + shared settings-panel builders), and `ui_collapsible.py` (`CollapsibleSection`), leaving `ui_widgets` as the leaf core (tooltips, toasts, event bindings).

### Fixed
- **Cast Timer no longer flashes a huge bogus estimate during lag or cast interrupts.** The timer-only overlay now samples the clock and cast progress on the same engine frame instead of on a free-running interval, so a stutter can't pair a fresh clock with stale progress and spike the estimate.
